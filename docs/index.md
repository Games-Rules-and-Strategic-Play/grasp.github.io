# Games, Rules, and Strategic Play (GRASP) Lab

Welcome to the **GRASP Lab**, an international research initiative based at the University of Primorska (UP FAMNIT) in Koper, Slovenia, working in close collaboration with partners worldwide, including Rhodes College and Rutgers University. 

Our lab explores the mathematical structures underlying sequential games of perfect information, positioning our work at the vibrant intersection of **Combinatorial Game Theory (CGT)**, **structural graph theory**, and **discrete mathematics**.

---

## Core Research Themes

### 🎲 Games on Integer Partitions
We study impartial games played on integer partitions and Young diagrams, analyzing how deep algebraic and combinatorial invariants dictate optimal play. Active areas include the structural mechanics of **LCTR** (*Left-Count, Top-Remove*), impartial chess on partitions, and the classification of Sato-Welter games through combinatorial hooks and self-conjugate partitions.

### 🔢 Advanced Variations of Nim
Taking inspiration from classical Nim, we extend strategic play into multi-dimensional spaces and hypergraphs. Our research includes **Hypergraph NIM**—where tokens are removed from multiple heaps simultaneously according to uniform hypergraph rules—and systematic classifications of **PET subtraction symmetric games** across various complexity boundaries.

### 📊 Grid-Based & Matrix Games
We analyze games played on bounded discrete grids, lattices, and matrices, tracking optimal movement strategies restricted by row and column boundaries. This includes structural observations of the **Column-Row game** and the mechanics of **Row impartial terminus**.

### 💻 Computational Complexity & Sprague-Grundy Theory
We bridge pure mathematics and theoretical computer science by algorithmically classifying games into precise complexity classes (such as polynomial-time solvable vs. PSPACE-complete). This cross-cutting methodological focus centers on calculating exact **Sprague-Grundy ($\mathcal{G}$) values** and proving their periodicity.
